# chapter eight: the house they never found

the field beyond the bridge was quiet compared with the village. the fire still roared behind them, but its sound seemed distant, as if it belonged to another world.

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] knelt in the grass with [[Iris|iris]] in his arms. his coat was beneath her head, and his hands were stained with blood he could not stop.

he kept telling her they would find help. there was a town beyond the northern road. there would be a doctor. they could still leave together, just as they had planned.

[[Iris|iris]] listened without interrupting him.

when he fell silent, she looked at the sky. the clouds above [[Hollow Creek|hollow creek]] glowed orange.

"do you remember the house?" she asked.

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] swallowed. "which house?"

"the one we were going to find."

he had imagined it so many times that it felt more real than the field beneath them.

"yes," he said. "a little house near a quiet road. there's a garden in front, and a tree beside the window."

"what kind of tree?"

"an apple tree."

"you hate apples."

"i can tolerate an apple tree."

she gave a faint laugh, and he held on to the sound as if it were something he could keep safe.

"and [[The Library|the library]]?" she asked.

"we'll make room for your books."

"all of them?"

"we'll build extra shelves."

"you will build them?"

"i suppose i will."

"they will be crooked."

"then you can complain about them every morning."

[[Iris|iris]] smiled, but the expression faded quickly.

"that sounds nice," she whispered.

"it will be nice. you will see."

she looked at him, and he knew she understood. she knew he was trying to make a promise neither of them could keep.

"[[Raven Blackwood|raven]], i'm sorry."

"don't say that."

"i wanted to see the world. i wanted to read all the books i could find. i wanted to see the sea. i wanted to live somewhere nobody knew our names."

"you will."

"i wanted to do those things with you."

his throat tightened. he bent over her hand and pressed it against his lips.

"we still can. just hold on a little longer."

[[Iris|iris]] shook her head slightly.

"you don't have to pretend with me."

he could not answer.

"you know what i liked about you?" she asked.

"your terrible sense of humor?"

"that was a flaw."

"one of many."

she smiled again, but the expression faded.

"you never asked me to be less curious. you never told me to stop asking questions. you let me be myself, even when i was annoying."

"you were always annoying."

"i know."

he tried to laugh, but the sound broke apart.

"you made me believe i could be something other than what i was born to be," he said. "you made me think i could have a home."

"you can."

"not without you."

[[Iris|iris]]'s fingers tightened weakly around his.

"you can still be kind, [[Raven Blackwood|raven]]. even when it hurts. especially when it hurts."

he shook his head, refusing to hear the farewell in her voice.

"please don't leave me."

for the first time since he had known her, [[Iris|iris]] had no joke to answer him with. she looked at him with all the tenderness she had spent so long pretending not to show.

"i love you," she whispered.

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] bent close to her. "i love you, [[Iris|iris]]. i love you."

she took one shallow breath, then another.

"tell me about the garden," she said.

so he did. he told her about the apple tree and the path from the gate to the door. he described a window where morning light would fall across her books. he told her there would be flowers along the fence, even though he knew nothing about gardening and would probably plant them in the wrong place.

[[Iris|iris]] listened with her eyes half closed.

"and the shelves?" she murmured.

"crooked," he said. "all of them."

"good."

her hand grew still in his.

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] continued speaking for a few moments before he understood that she was no longer listening. he called her name. he touched her cheek. he held her closer and begged her to open her eyes, though a part of him already knew she would not.

the night gave him no answer.

for a long time, he stayed beside her in the grass. he did not care that the fire was fading or that the sky was slowly turning gray. he did not care that the village behind him was becoming a place of ash and broken stone.

eventually, he noticed the silver [[Margaret’s Pendant|pendant]] at her throat. the chain had come loose during the escape. with shaking fingers, he unclasped it and held it in his palm.

he had the ring his mother had left him. now he had the [[Margaret’s Pendant|pendant]] [[Iris|iris]] had worn every day. two small pieces of silver. two people he had loved. two reminders that kindness could exist in a world that seemed determined to destroy it.

when dawn came, [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] rose. he carried [[Iris|iris]] to a quiet place beneath a tree, away from the road. he buried her there as best he could, using his hands and a broken piece of wood. he placed a flat stone above the earth and sat beside it until the sun had risen.

then he turned toward the road.

he did not look back at [[Hollow Creek|hollow creek]].

he left the village behind, carrying the [[Margaret’s Pendant|pendant]] in his pocket and the house they had imagined in his memory. he would live for years, then decades, then centuries. people would come and go. towns would change. children would grow old, and the stories told about [[Vampire Lore|vampires]] would continue.

some would call him a monster. some would call him a mystery. none would know about [[Iris|iris]] miller, or the way she laughed when he tried to make a joke, or how she could turn an ordinary afternoon into something worth remembering.

none would know about the house with the apple tree.

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] never became his father. he never stopped believing humans and [[Vampire Lore|vampires]] could live beside one another. but he never found the house he and [[Iris|iris]] had promised to share, and he never stopped imagining what it might have been like to hear her complaining about his crooked shelves.

he had lived for a hundred years before he met [[Iris|iris]].

he would live for hundreds more without her.

but no amount of time would ever be enough to forget the home they never found.
