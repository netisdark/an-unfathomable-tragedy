# chapter three: partners in trouble

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] returned to [[The Library|the library]] three days later.

"you came back," [[Iris|iris]] said. "i was beginning to think the books had eaten you."

"they were less troublesome than expected."

"you must have found the one that bites."

he returned every book clean and undamaged. [[Iris|iris]] inspected them with exaggerated suspicion.

"remarkable. not a single stain."

"i told you i don't drink tea."

"perhaps you are secretly a well-behaved person."

"please don't spread such rumors."

he stayed to help her rearrange a shelf. [[Iris|iris]] changed her mind about where the books belonged so many times that [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] finally asked whether she expected the books to follow her thoughts.

"they should try harder," she said.

after that, he found reasons to visit. sometimes he borrowed books. sometimes he returned them early. once, he brought a wooden box he had found outside the [[The Boarding House|boarding house]], hoping to locate its owner. inside was a silver pocket watch, which they traced to a baker named mr. ellis.

the baker thanked them and offered a loaf of bread. [[Iris|iris]] accepted two.

"you only found one watch," [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] said as they left.

"and he only gave us one loaf. i corrected the imbalance."

"you stole half his bread."

"i rescued it from a life of loneliness."

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] shook his head, but he smiled.

it became a pattern. [[Iris|iris]] noticed things others ignored, and [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] quietly solved the problems that followed. when a shopkeeper accused a poor boy of stealing an apple, [[Iris|iris]] asked questions until the truth came out. when an old woman lost her purse, [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] found it beneath a loose board near the market. when a widow was mocked, [[Iris|iris]] confronted the people responsible so fiercely that [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] had to guide her away before the argument became a fight.

"you cannot challenge every person who says something foolish," he told her.

"why not?"

"because there are too many of them."

"that is a terrible reason to stop."

"it is practical."

"you must be exhausting at parties."

"i don't attend parties."

"i had guessed."

they learned each other's habits. [[Iris|iris]] tapped her fingers when thinking and collected scraps of paper with interesting words. she could never walk past a stray cat without trying to befriend it. [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] preferred quiet rooms, counted exits when entering a building, and looked toward windows whenever evening approached.

one rainy afternoon, they took shelter in an abandoned shed while carrying donated books to a family on the edge of town.

"do you ever get tired of being alone?" [[Iris|iris]] asked.

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] looked at her. "what makes you think i'm alone?"

"you never talk about your family. you eat by yourself. and you look as if you expect every person you meet to stab you."

"perhaps i've met too many people."

"perhaps you've met the wrong people."

he looked toward the rain. "i had a family once. it ended badly."

[[Iris|iris]] did not ask for details. "i'm sorry."

"you don't even know what happened."

"i don't need to know everything to be sorry you were hurt."

the simplicity of it unsettled him more than any question could.

she offered him a paper packet of bread. their fingers brushed, and he pulled his hand away too quickly. [[Iris|iris]] noticed but pretended not to.

on the walk back, they passed [[The Forest|the forest]]. a dark shape moved between the trees, too quick to identify.

[[Raven Blackwood|raven]] stopped.

"what is it?" [[Iris|iris]] asked.

"nothing. we should go."

his voice had changed. [[Iris|iris]] did not argue. she walked beside him until they reached the first houses.

that night, [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] stood at his window and watched [[The Forest|the forest]]. he knew the shape he had seen. it was not an animal. someone from his old world had come close enough to watch the village.

for the first time since arriving in [[Hollow Creek|hollow creek]], he wondered whether his past had followed him. and for the first time, he feared his attempt at an ordinary life might put someone else in danger.

someone whose laughter had begun to make the world feel less empty.
