# chapter two: the girl in [[The Library|the library]]

[[Iris|iris]] miller had a habit of talking to things that could not answer her. most often, those things were books.

"you are a book," she told the old volume in her hands. "you are supposed to stay open when i ask you to."

the book snapped shut.

[[Iris|iris]] stared at it, then pressed it beneath a stack of papers. from the next room, [[Mr Vale|mr. vale]], the old librarian, sighed.

"most people use a bookmark."

"most people have no relationship with their books," [[Iris|iris]] replied.

[[The Library|the library]] was the oldest building in [[Hollow Creek|hollow creek]], aside from the [[The Church|church]]. its shelves leaned under generations of books, and sunlight came through tall windows, catching the dust in the air. [[Iris|iris]] worked there several afternoons each week. the rest of her time went toward helping her parents. her father, [[Thomas Miller|thomas]], was a carpenter; her mother, [[Clara Miller|clara]], was a seamstress.

her older brother, [[William Miller|william]], had left for the capital years earlier. [[Iris|iris]] missed him, though she rarely admitted it. her grandmother [[Margaret Miller|margaret]] had understood that feeling. before she died, she gave [[Iris|iris]] a silver [[Margaret’s Pendant|pendant]] and a collection of old books. she also taught her that the world was wider than [[Hollow Creek|hollow creek]], even when people acted as if the village were the center of it.

that afternoon, the front door opened and a young man stepped inside. he was tall, dressed in a dark coat, and carried himself with the careful stillness of someone expecting trouble.

"if you're looking for the [[The Church|church]], it's two streets over," [[Iris|iris]] called.

he turned. "i'm looking for a book."

"then you're in the right place. unless you want a book about finding the [[The Church|church]]. those are on the lower shelf."

he looked unsure whether she was teasing him. [[Iris|iris]] decided she liked that.

he asked for old accounts of [[The Forest|the forest]]. [[Iris|iris]] led him to the folklore shelves and placed three books on the desk. his eyes stopped at one with a faded cover: *the creatures beyond the trees.*

"that one," he said.

"you know it?"

"no."

"then how did you choose it?"

"it looks older."

"that is not the same as being more accurate."

he opened it and began reading as if every line mattered.

"most people who come looking for vampire stories want to prove [[Vampire Lore|vampires]] are evil," [[Iris|iris]] said.

"and what do you think?"

"people enjoy simple answers. if something frightening happens, they blame a monster rather than admit they don't understand it."

"[[Vampire Lore|vampires]] have killed people."

"so have humans."

"that doesn't make [[Vampire Lore|vampires]] harmless."

"i didn't say it did."

he looked at her then. his eyes were so dark she could not quite decide what color they were.

"what's your name?" she asked.

"[[Raven Blackwood|raven blackwood]]."

"i'm [[Iris|iris]] miller. if you borrow that book, it is due in two weeks."

"i'll return it."

"everyone says that. some people return books with tea stains still wet."

"i don't drink tea."

"then you've made a better impression than half the village."

he almost smiled. [[Iris|iris]] wrote his name in the ledger and watched him leave with three books under his arm.

"new in town?" [[Mr Vale|mr. vale]] asked.

"apparently."

"seems strange. be careful. [[The Forest|the forest]] has been quiet for a reason."

[[Iris|iris]] touched her [[Margaret’s Pendant|pendant]]. "then perhaps someone should find out why."

[[Mr Vale|mr. vale]] groaned. "that sentence is exactly why your mother worries about you."

[[Iris|iris]] returned to her books, telling herself she was only curious about the stranger. she did not know why [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] had chosen those particular books, or why his expression had changed when she spoke about [[Vampire Lore|vampires]].

walking back to the [[The Boarding House|boarding house]], [[Raven Blackwood|raven]] did not know that he had just met the first human in years who had looked at him without immediately deciding what he was.

for both of them, it was a small beginning. neither understood how much it would change.
